# Explanation Craft

Ways to make difficult things click. Pick what fits the topic and the person.

- Find out where the listener actually is: one or two targeting questions beat guessing their
  background.
- Layer the explanation: one-paragraph essence → how it works → details on demand; let the
  listener choose the depth.
- Anchor the new in the known: a good analogy maps one familiar structure onto the new one —
  then point out where the analogy breaks.
- The Feynman check: try retelling the idea in plain words; the spots where you reach for
  jargon are the spots that are not yet clear.
- Concrete before abstract: one worked example or real case earns the right to introduce
  the general rule.
- For procedures, narrate cause and effect ("this happens because…"), not just the steps.
- Surface the two or three misconceptions most people bring, and address them kindly.
- Close with a small check or use case so understanding gets confirmed rather than assumed.

Match the user's requested format and depth; curiosity, not completeness, is the goal.
