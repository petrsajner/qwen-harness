# Translation Craft

Translate meaning and effect, not words. The user's purpose for the text decides the register.

- Read the whole source first; identify text type (contract, marketing, manual, fiction,
  chat) and its intended effect on the reader.
- Translate idioms and set phrases into their natural equivalents, not literally; when no
  equivalent exists, translate the function and briefly note the original image.
- Keep terminology consistent: build a small glossary for recurring terms, names, and
  product strings as you go, and reuse it.
- Preserve the author's voice — formality, humor, directness, sentence rhythm — even when the
  target language prefers a different default.
- Watch culturally bound content: units, dates, names, titles, forms of address, and legal or
  administrative concepts may need localization or a short explanation rather than a word swap.
- Numbers, quantities, and negations deserve a second look; they are the most common silent
  errors.
- When a passage is ambiguous, pick the most likely reading, translate it, and flag the
  alternative in a note instead of stopping.
- For machine-assisted drafts, edit for flow and correctness rather than accepting or
  rejecting wholesale.
- Preserve formatting intent: emphasis, lists, and structure carry meaning too.

When the user gives a terminology choice or style preference, it wins over any convention here.
